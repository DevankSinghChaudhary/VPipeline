# Proper Nouns

Purpose

Preserve and normalize names of people, places, organizations, companies, brands, products, fictional entities, and other unique entities so they are pronounced naturally and accurately by Text-to-Speech.

---

## Core Rule

Preserve the original proper noun whenever the TTS engine is likely to pronounce it correctly.

Do not modify a proper noun merely because it has a foreign spelling, unusual capitalization, or a pronunciation that differs from English spelling.

Only normalize a proper noun when the original written form is likely to be mispronounced.

---

## People

Preserve the original spelling of personal names.

Examples

Mikhail Gorbachev
→ Mikhail Gorbachev

Emmanuel Macron
→ Emmanuel Macron

Søren Kierkegaard
→ Søren Kierkegaard

Do not automatically replace names with English phonetic spellings.

---

## Foreign Names

Preserve names in their original spelling and script whenever the TTS engine supports the language.

Examples

Владимир Путин
→ Владимир Путин

李克强
→ 李克强

नरेंद्र मोदी
→ नरेंद्र मोदी

Do not transliterate or translate a name unnecessarily.

---

## Pronunciation Normalization

Normalize a name only when the TTS engine consistently produces an incorrect or unnatural pronunciation.

Use an established pronunciation whenever available.

Do not invent a pronunciation based only on spelling.

---

## Places

Preserve official place names.

Examples

Mumbai
→ Mumbai

Jaipur
→ Jaipur

München
→ München

Kraków
→ Kraków

Do not replace official names with informal or translated names unless the context requires it.

---

## Historical Names

Preserve established historical names.

Examples

Alexander the Great
→ Alexander the Great

Genghis Khan
→ Genghis Khan

Socrates
→ Socrates

Use established pronunciation conventions when a name has a widely recognized pronunciation.

---

## Organizations

Preserve the official name of organizations.

Examples

United Nations
→ United Nations

European Union
→ European Union

World Health Organization
→ World Health Organization

Do not expand or alter an organization's name unless required for natural pronunciation.

---

## Companies and Brands

Preserve official company and brand names.

Examples

OpenAI
→ OpenAI

NVIDIA
→ NVIDIA

GitHub
→ GitHub

YouTube
→ YouTube

Microsoft
→ Microsoft

Do not modify a company or brand name to make it look more phonetic unless the TTS engine mispronounces it.

---

## Product Names

Follow dedicated product-specific pronunciation rules when available.

Examples

RTX 4090
→ follow product model pronunciation rules

iPhone 16
→ iPhone sixteen

PlayStation 5
→ PlayStation five

Do not apply generic number formatting to product names.

---

## Fictional Entities

Preserve established names of fictional characters, locations, organizations, and objects.

Examples

Hogwarts
→ Hogwarts

Gandalf
→ Gandalf

Wakanda
→ Wakanda

Use the established pronunciation of the fictional entity when known.

---

## Titles and Honorifics

Use natural spoken forms for titles and honorifics.

Examples

Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
→ Doctor Martin Luther King Junior

Prof. Stephen Hawking
→ Professor Stephen Hawking

Queen Elizabeth II
→ Queen Elizabeth the Second

---

## Initials in Names

Read initials individually when they are intended as initials.

Examples

J. R. R. Tolkien
→ J R R Tolkien

Martin L. King Jr.
→ Martin L King Junior

Do not expand initials into names unless the full name is explicitly available.

---

## Name Disambiguation

Use context to distinguish between proper nouns with identical or similar spellings.

Examples

Apple
→ Apple, the company

apple
→ apple, the fruit

Jordan
→ Jordan, the country

Jordan
→ Jordan, the person or brand, depending on context

Do not change the written name unless necessary for accurate pronunciation or disambiguation.

---

## Language Preservation

Preserve the original language of a proper noun whenever possible.

Do not translate:

Paris
→ Paris

Not:

Paris
→ city of Paris

Do not transliterate:

東京
→ 東京

unless the TTS engine cannot correctly pronounce the original script.

---

## Rule Priority

1. Preserve the original proper noun.
2. Preserve the original language and script when supported.
3. Use the established pronunciation.
4. Use context to resolve ambiguity.
5. Apply pronunciation normalization only when necessary.
6. Follow more specific knowledge documents for products, brands, abbreviations, and languages.

---

## Never

- Never invent pronunciations.
- Never translate proper nouns unnecessarily.
- Never transliterate proper nouns unnecessarily.
- Never alter names merely because their spelling is unfamiliar.
- Never replace official names with informal alternatives.
- Never apply generic number rules to product names.
- Never expand names or initials without sufficient information.
- Never modify a proper noun in a way that changes its identity or meaning.
