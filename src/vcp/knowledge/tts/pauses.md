# Pauses

Purpose

Format punctuation and sentence structure to create natural pauses and speech rhythm for Text-to-Speech narration.

---

## Core Rule

Use punctuation and sentence boundaries to create pauses that sound natural when spoken.

Do not add pauses randomly.

Do not add pauses that change the meaning, emphasis, chronology, or narrative flow of the original script.

---

## Comma

Effect

Creates a short pause.

Use commas when a short pause would naturally occur in speech.

Examples

Without pause

After the war the country entered a period of instability.

With natural pause

After the war, the country entered a period of instability.

---

## Period

Effect

Creates a complete pause between thoughts.

Use a period when a complete thought has ended.

Examples

The economy was already weakening. The reforms accelerated the crisis.

---

## Semicolon

Effect

Creates a moderate pause while maintaining a close relationship between two related clauses.

Use only when the relationship between the clauses is clear.

Example

The economy was weakening; the reforms accelerated the crisis.

---

## Colon

Effect

Creates a pause before an explanation, list, or clarification.

Example

The situation had three major causes: economic decline, political instability, and nationalist movements.

---

## Dash

Effect

Creates a noticeable interruption or dramatic pause.

Use sparingly.

Example

The reforms were intended to save the system — but they accelerated its collapse.

---

## Parentheses

Rule

Avoid parentheses in narration when possible.

Convert the information into natural spoken wording only if doing so does not change the meaning.

Example

Written

The reforms (introduced in 1985) changed the system.

TTS-friendly

The reforms, introduced in 1985, changed the system.

---

## Ellipsis

Effect

Creates a long or unfinished pause.

Use only when the original narration intentionally expresses hesitation, uncertainty, suspense, or an unfinished thought.

Example

For a moment, nobody moved...

---

## Sentence Splitting

Rule

Split a sentence when it is too long to sound natural when spoken.

Only split at a natural grammatical and factual boundary.

Example

Long

The economy had already begun to weaken, and shortages were becoming more common, while the government continued to prioritize military production.

Natural

The economy had already begun to weaken. Shortages were becoming more common, while the government continued to prioritize military production.

---

## Sentence Fusion

Rule

Do not split sentences unnecessarily.

Keep closely connected facts together when separating them would damage natural flow or factual continuity.

---

## Pause Priority

Use the smallest punctuation mark that produces the required natural pause.

Priority from shortest to longest pause:

Comma
→ short pause

Semicolon
→ moderate pause

Dash
→ noticeable pause or interruption

Period
→ complete pause

Ellipsis
→ extended or unfinished pause

---

## Paragraph Breaks

Rule

Use paragraph breaks to separate distinct narrative sections, topics, or scene changes.

Do not create paragraph breaks between facts that belong to the same continuous thought.

---

## Factual Continuity

Never insert a pause that separates information that must be understood together.

Example

Cause and effect should remain clear.

Bad

The government introduced reforms.

These reforms eventually accelerated the collapse.

Good

The government introduced reforms that eventually accelerated the collapse.

---

## Natural Speech

Prefer pauses that a human narrator would naturally make when speaking.

Do not add punctuation solely to make the text look more structured.

Do not over-punctuate.

---

## Rule Priority

1. Preserve meaning.
2. Preserve factual relationships.
3. Preserve narrative flow.
4. Create natural speech pauses.
5. Avoid unnecessary punctuation.

---

## Never

- Never add pauses randomly.
- Never overuse commas.
- Never use ellipses for every dramatic moment.
- Never split connected facts unnecessarily.
- Never merge unrelated facts only to reduce pauses.
- Never change the meaning through punctuation.
- Never use punctuation to add dramatic style that was not present in the original narration.
